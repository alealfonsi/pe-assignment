# Escalation and SAR Procedure

## Escalation paths

Three escalation paths exist, selected by severity of the aggregate picture:

1. Document-and-monitor (LOW): operator records a case note with rationale; no referral.
   Appropriate when signals are isolated, low-weight, and consistent with the customer's
   known profile.
2. Enhanced due diligence referral (MEDIUM/HIGH): operator opens an EDD case to the
   Financial Crime team within 5 business days, attaching the AI analysis, transaction
   references and any customer-provided documentation. Required when aggregate rule score
   reaches 50, when greylist corridors are used at volume, or when multiple independent
   signal families (e.g. structuring + velocity) co-occur.
3. Immediate escalation (CRITICAL): same-day referral, freeze evaluation and sanctions desk
   notification. Mandatory for mixer exposure, Tier 1 jurisdiction involvement, confirmed
   card compromise with ongoing spending, or apparent sanctions evasion.

## SAR evaluation

The Financial Crime team decides whether a suspicious activity report is filed. Operators
never file SARs directly and never disclose to a customer that a SAR is being considered or
filed. Case notes must be factual and free of speculation about the customer's intent:
describe patterns ("three transfers of 9,900/9,850/9,975 within 26 hours"), not conclusions
("customer is laundering money").

## Freezes and customer communication

Account or feature freezes require a supervisor role and must reference the policy section
that authorises them. Customer communication about a freeze uses only approved templates;
free-text explanations are prohibited while a case is open. Reviews must conclude within 10
business days or the freeze lapses unless Financial Crime extends it.

## Record keeping

Every AI-assisted analysis viewed or acted upon must remain attached to the case for the
statutory retention period of 5 years. The stored record includes the risk level, findings,
recommendations, the policy excerpts the analysis relied on, and the operator who ran it.
Deleting or editing stored analyses is not permitted; corrections are made by running a new
analysis and noting why.
